About

Research that is built to be used in a budget decision.

I am an agricultural and development economist with fourteen years of experience across research institutes, the United Nations, a university and the centre of government. My work measures what public support to agriculture achieves: whether it raises productivity, at what cost, and for whom.

I work with large farm and household surveys, and I have also sat on the other side of the table, directing the delivery of presidential priority programmes in Burkina Faso through two political transitions. That combination shapes how I write: for the minister, the grants committee and the journal referee alike.

I work in French and English, and I speak Moore, Dioula and Malinke.

Productivity and public spending

Total factor productivity, stochastic frontiers and public-expenditure analysis that show which support measures pay off.

Impact evaluation

Randomized and quasi-experimental evaluations of subsidies, seed vouchers, extension, irrigation and climate services, with five published evaluations.

Jobs in agrifood systems

How many jobs investments create for young people, at what cost, and whether those jobs provide a reliable income.

Evidence in government

Results frameworks, delivery units and monitoring systems that turn analysis into completed programmes.
